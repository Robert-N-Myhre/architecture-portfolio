# About

I'm Robert N. Myhre, an independent technology researcher with a background in enterprise infrastructure and architecture.

I investigate questions about systems: what they actually do, how components interact, where constraints emerge, and what happens when assumptions meet implementation. My interests range across GPU systems and distributed AI, networking, security, automation, and governed agentic systems.

My technical background informs which questions I notice. The investigations here are ones I chose to pursue.

## Why I investigate

Curiosity often starts with a small discrepancy: a feature that should work but does not; a topology diagram that seems to promise a performance outcome; a platform abstraction that might reduce complexity; or an AI workflow whose authority boundary is not yet clear.

I might build a prototype, capture measurements, draw an architecture, or work through a set of constraints. I try to record what I believed at the start, which claims the evidence actually supports, and what changed my mind.

A completed implementation is not the definition of success. Discovering that an idea does not work as expected can be just as useful. Some conceptual investigations may remain unproven.

## Areas that interest me

- AI infrastructure, GPUs, topology, distributed inference, and observability
- Human–AI collaboration, communication, and agency
- Networking and distributed systems
- Identity, security, trust boundaries, and governed automation
- Cloud and infrastructure platforms
- Emerging ideas that raise worthwhile technical questions

## Background

My professional foundation is in networking and infrastructure architecture, supported by hands-on engineering, technical writing, and teaching. That experience supplies context for the research but is not its subject.
